What counts as a “red herring” here (by your definition)

A candidate red herring in QM/QFT would be something that:

is mathematically consistent and operationally successful

answers many how questions

but may distract from, or postpone, answering why this structure at all?

and whose prominence may be a historical or pedagogical artifact

With that in mind, here are several serious candidates, ranked roughly from “safe to say” to “philosophically radioactive.”

1. The wavefunction as an ontic object

Why it may be a red herring

The formalism treats the wavefunction as:

central

evolving

measurable (via tomography)

collapsible

Yet:

it lives in configuration space, not spacetime

it is not directly observable

it is redundantly representable (global phase, gauge freedom)

it encodes probabilities, not events

The red-herring possibility is this:

We may be mistaking a computational bookkeeper for a physical entity.

This is not an anti-wavefunction claim — it’s the suggestion that ontic debates about “what the wavefunction really is” may be downstream of a deeper misidentification.

Tell-tale symptom
Endless metaphysical debate with no experimental discriminator.

2. Unitary time evolution as fundamental

Unitary evolution is treated as sacrosanct:

reversible

information-preserving

linear

universal

Collapse, measurement, decoherence, etc. are then forced to conform.

The red-herring suspicion:

We may be privileging unitarity because it makes the math tractable and elegant, not because nature demands it globally.

In other words:

unitarity may be contextual, not absolute

insisting on it everywhere may be what creates the measurement problem

This aligns tightly with your Chapter 5 theme: ignoring may be the skill we lack.

3. Hilbert space dimensionality as “given”

In QM and especially QFT, Hilbert space is:

infinite-dimensional

fixed in advance

silently accepted

But very little is said about:

why this space

why this factorization

why tensor product structure appears “natural”

A red-herring possibility:

We may be solving dynamics inside a space whose structure already presupposes the answer.

This becomes acute in:

entanglement discussions

subsystem decomposition

observer / system splits

If the choice of Hilbert space is doing the real work, then focusing on dynamics inside it may be misdirection.

4. QFT’s obsession with local fields

In QFT, locality is enforced by:

field operators at spacetime points

microcausality

local commutators

Yet:

entanglement is nonlocal

measurement outcomes are global

renormalization smears locality across scales

The red-herring suspicion:

Local fields may be a representational convenience, not a fundamental ontology.

Fields work extraordinarily well — but they may be the wrong primitives, especially for measurement and collapse questions.

5. Renormalization as “resolution” rather than warning

Renormalization is often presented as a triumph:

infinities tamed

predictions rescued

calculations stabilized

But from a red-herring perspective:

Renormalization may be nature telling us we are asking the wrong questions at the wrong scale.

Treating it as a solved problem may have delayed deeper inquiry into:

scale-dependence of laws

emergent versus fundamental degrees of freedom

whether “bare parameters” exist at all

6. Information as a substitute for ontology (most dangerous)

Modern discourse often says:

“QM is about information”

“Collapse is just an update”

“The quantum state is epistemic”

This can be clarifying — but also evasive.

The red-herring risk:

Replacing ontology with information talk may dissolve the problem linguistically without resolving it physically.

Information about what?
Relative to what?
Stored where?

If “information” becomes a placeholder, it may function exactly like a red herring: soothing, flexible, and ultimately non-explanatory.

Meta-pattern (this is the key insight)

Across all these candidates, a common structure appears:

Mathematical success + conceptual ambiguity → attention sinks

The formalism works so well that:

questioning its primitives feels unnecessary

anomalies are treated as interpretation problems

the selection of structure is no longer interrogated

That is exactly the cognitive pattern Red Herrings train against.

Why this dovetails with PoP

Your work consistently presses on:

indistinguishability

self-reference

ignored degrees of freedom

premature commitments to structure

Seen through that lens, many QM/QFT “foundations debates” may be:

debates inside a red herring

rather than debates about which herring to ignore

Bottom line

Yes — there are credible, high-level red-herring candidates in QM and QFT.

Not mistakes.
Not failures.
But attention traps.

And the most telling sign is this:

The measurement problem persists not because we lack math, but because we may be asking exquisitely precise questions about the wrong things.
