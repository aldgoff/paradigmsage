# COMICS-PORTENTS.md

# Intent
 
  The consistent use of comics throughout this work enforces a single, underappreciated claim: **self-reference is everywhere**.

  Humans are remarkably good at detecting self-reference. We intuitively sense the incongruities it produces—through humor, irony, puns, paradox, karma, and recursion—often without confusion, but almost always without recognizing either its ubiquity or its significance. We feel it, laugh at it, and move on.

  This project deliberately exploits that faculty.

  The opening comics are not illustrations of the chapters that follow. They are **portents**. Each one trains the reader’s attention toward a particular *kind* of self-reference before any formal discussion begins. The goal is not explanation, but acclimation: to make self-reference familiar rather than alien, expected rather than exceptional.

  Self-reference is largely excluded from formal scientific discourse, not because it is rare or unimportant, but because we have never properly formulated it. Yet measurement, observation, and explanation all involve systems that, at some level, refer to themselves. The central argument of this work is that without confronting self-reference explicitly, the quantum measurement problem cannot be resolved.

  In this sense, the comics weaponize an intuitive human capability and slowly drag it—reluctantly but deliberately—into physics.

# Act I

 ## Chapter 01 - Paradigms
  - **Comic:** Unknown author: unknown title. a single panel cartoon, black and white.
  - **Desc:** Two grunts are pushing a wagon on four square wagon wheels, carrying a whole set of round wagon wheels.
  - **Role:** Paradigms can be opaque on the front end, but embarressingly obvious on the back end.

 ## Chapter 02 - Paradox
  - **Comic:** Fox Trot: "Logic". Four panel cartoon, black and white.
  - **Desc:** The young kid (family genius) has a question requring parental approval.
  1st panel: "Mom, can Marcus sleep over tonight?" "It's ok with me if it's ok with your father." 
  2nd panel: "Dad, can Marcus sleep over tonight?" "It's ok with me if it's ok with your mother."
  3rd panel: Kid lost in thought, perplexed.
  4th panel: Kid with nose in book "Logic", on phone, "Marcus, are you still there?"
  - **Role:** Self-reference has been largely ignored by research into logic, but clearly supports both paradox and indeterminacy.

  ## Chapter 03 - Special Relativity
  - **Comic:** Frank & Ernest: "Counterintuitive". Single-panel cartoon, black and white.
  - **Desc:** Two cavemen around a fire, one says "You make it by rubbing two sticks together - counterintutive isn't it?
  - **Role:** Primes the reader to accept that correct physical descriptions may violate everyday intuition, even when well established.

  ## Chapter 04 - Quantum Mechanics
  - **Comic:** xkcd: "Amber Particles of Grain". Single-panel cartoon, colored.
  - **Desc:** A couple standing in a field of ripe wheat. She says, "Well, when we observe them, they become amber *particles* of grain."
  - **Role:** Primes the reader for the central role of observation in quantum mechanics and the collapse of categories under measurement.

 ## Chapter 05 - Uncertainty
  - **Comic:** Wilkinson, “Estrogen Studies”. Six-panel cartoon, black and white.
  - **Desc:** A news report alternately links estrogen to reducing and causing heart attacks. 
  A woman reacts decisively to each headline in turn, with catastrophic results. 
  The anchor concludes by noting that scientists are now studying estrogen studies themselves.
  - **Role:** Primes the reader to distrust isolated conclusions and to recognize feedback between measurement, interpretation, and outcome.

 ## Chapter 06 - Interference
  - **Comic:** FoxTrot, “Watched”. Two-panel lead-in panels only, color.
  - **Desc:** Jason walks away from school, nervously looking over his shoulder, asking why he always feels like he’s being watched.
  - **Role:** Breaks the fourth wall to highlight observer participation; primes the reader for interference effects arising from observation itself.

 ## Chapter 07 - Entanglement
  - **Comic:** Speed Bump, “Plagiarism”. Single-panel cartoon, black and white.
  - **Desc:** Two prisoners sit side by side discussing their crimes; both wear identical prisoner ID numbers on their uniforms.
    Inmate #1, "Grand theft auto, you?"
    Inmate #2. "Plagiarism."
  - **Role:** Self-reference can be subtle, indirect, and context sensitive.

 ## Chapter 08 - QTP
  - **Comic:** Frank & Ernest, “Global”. Single-panel cartoon, black and white.
  - **Desc:** Ernest sits depressed beneath a sign reading “Flat Earth Society HQ.” Frank notes that Ernest has been unhappy ever since their membership went global.
  - **Role:** Primes the reader to recognize how success can undermine the foundations of an idea by exposing hidden contradictions.

 ## Chapter 09 - R&D
  - **Comic:** Frank & Ernest: “Procrastination". Single-panel cartoon, black and white.
  - **Desc:** Frank and Ernest in an art gallery, sculpture exhibit.
  They are standing in front of a large cube of granite, titled, "“Procrastination”.
   - **Role:** The art can be in the labeling as well as in the craft. Procrastination can be a viable scientific strategy.

# Act II

 ## Chapter 10 - Objections
  - **Comic:** Frant & Ernest: "Klepto". Single-panel cartoon, black and white.
  - **Desc:** Sign outside the Kleptomania Clinic is missing a letter.
  - **Role:** Irony, the comic refers to itself indirectly.

 ## Chapter 11 - QT3
  - **Comic:** BC: "Boomerang". Tri-panel cartoon, black and white.
  - **Desc:** Customer approaches gift exchange 'rock' holding a boomerang.
  "I'd like to return this boomerang, it doesn't work."
  BC, standing behind the rock, "I'm sorry, faulty boomerangs are non-returnable."
  - **Role:** A pun that exploits ambiguity between physical and commercial meanings, 
  illustrating how a system can remain internally consistent while producing an intuitively unjust outcome.

 ## Chapter 12 - By Contradiction
  - **Comic:** Luann: "Horoscope". Six-panel cartoon, colored.
  - **Desc:** Luann and best friend are arguing about the validity of horoscopes.
  Launn is reading it and it predicts they will have a fight tomorrow.
  Best friend is skeptical and derisive, proving her point by noting that having predicted tomorrow's fight they can avoid it.
  Their disagreement spirals into a fight, today.
  But then Luann notices the horoscope she is reading is from yesterday's paper. Oops.
  - **Role:** Is a predicted future escapable?
  Does foreknowledge allow avoidance—or do contradictions fail because we quietly assumed the wrong future in the first place?

 ## Chapter 13 - 12 Light Seconds
  - **Comic:** Fox Trot: "Football Pattern". Four-panel cartoon, colored.
  - **Desc:** Older brother tries to teach a football route to his younger (genius) brother.
  Instead of running the route, he runs the hypotenuse. Same thing in geometry, not the same thing in football.
  - **Role:** Context determines whether two things are comensurate or not.

 ## Chapter 14 - World Ribbons
  - **Comic:** Ziggy: "Hard to Think Outside the Box". Single panel cartoon, black and white.
  - **Desc:** Ziggy, head in hands, "It's really hard to think outside the box, when you're a single-panel cartoon!"
  - **Role:** You can think outside the box in one domain, and then another, but miss their overlap.

 ## Chapter 15 - Symmetric Spacetime Intervals
  - **Comic:** Far Side: "My Brain is Full". Single panel cartoon, black and white.
  - **Desc:** A classroom. One student has his had up asking a question, 
  "Mr. Osborne can I be excused, my brain is full."
  Upon closer examination, his head is much smaller than any other students'.
  - **Role:** As act II progresses, the technical material becomes more demanding.
  This comic commiserates with the Intrepid Reader.
  - **Self-Reference:** The student's physical appearence leds credence to a classic line that in most contexts has little merit.

 ## Chapter 16 - Bowtie
  - **Comic:** Escher: "Drawing Hands". A black and white etching.
  - **Desc:** An etching of two hands each drawing the other, emerging into 3D out of the flat surface of the drawing.
  - **Role:** Self-reference is widespread; it is not limited to just logic, math and physics, but can showup even in art.
  Self-reference can be self-consistent

 ## Chapter 17 - Teleportation
  - **Comic:** Rose is Rose: "Deceptive Checkup". Multi-panel cartoon in color.
  - **Desc:** Wife enlists husband's help in conning their kitten into the pet carrier so they can take it to the vet.
  The twist is she is actually conning the husband into the car to go for a medical checkup, the kitten was red-herring.
  - **Role:** Nature is deceptive, we need to stay on our toes, or we'll end up somewhere other than where intended.

 ## Chapter 18 - Indistinguishable
  - **Comic:** Show, "Conundrum". Three panel cartoon, black and white.
  - **Desc:** Wise old bird quips, "Life is a conundrum, Skyler."
  Skyler, "I don't understand..."
  Wise old bird, "Precisely."
  - **Role:** Quantum mechanics is a conundrum, one for more deep, intricated, and counter-intuitive than we supposed.

 ## Chapter 19 - Quantum Optics 
  - **Comic:** xkcd, Analagies. Four-panel cartoon, black and white.
  - **Desc:** Convoluted conversation defining analogies, metaphors, and similes in terms of analogies, metaphors and similes.
  - **Role:** Convoluted, hard to follow self-reference about English words we mostly use wrongly.

 ## Chapter 20 - FTL by Indistinguishability
  - **Comic:** Frazz, "Hypothetically Speaking". Single-panel cartoon, colored.
  - **Desc:** Youngster speaking to oldster, "Let's say we were talking about something hypothetically..."
  - **Role:** The speaker is claiming a hypothetical scenario, but the audience suspects the topic is anything but hypothetical.

 ## Chapter 21 - Now and Then
  - **Comic:** Frank & Ernest, "Inexperienced Loan Officer". Single-panel cartoon, black and white.
  - **Desc:** Frant and Ernest are at the loan department of the local bank.
  They ask the clerk, "Do you have an inexperienced young loan officer in need of a valuable lesson?"
  - **Role:** The query is self-denying.
  By asking it they reveal the odds are they will never repay the loan, and thus should not be given one.

## Chapter 22 - QTI
  - **Comic:** Mr. Spot, "Borrow from the Future". Six-panel cartoon, black and white.
  - **Desc:** Uses the spatial adjancy of the panels, and their implied temporal ordering, to let present-self steal money from future self.
  - **Role:** Shows temporal indeterminacy, but begs the question of where did the money come from in the first case.

 ## Chapter 23 - Critique
  - **Comic:** The Far Side, "Beach Boards". Single-panel cartoon, black and white.
  - **Desc:** Beach scene, two dweebs with whiteboards covered in equations.
  The beach babes are currenlty behind the dweeb with the smaller whiteboard, 
  but starting to flirt with the other dweeb, who has apparently just arrived on the scence.
  - **Role:** Inverts the stereotype of the muscular beach bum who attracts the bikini babes.

 ## Chapter 24 - Frame
  - **Comic:** Mother Goose and Grimm, "God Sneezed". Single-panel, black and white
  - **Desc:** A heavenly scene. Narrator, "Suddenly there was stunned silience in heaven, no one knew what to say when God sneezed."
  - **Role:** Pivots on the polite phrase, "God bless you" when someone sneezes. 
  Communicates a situation where there is a lack of clarity about the next actions.

 ## Chapter 25 - Laws of Form
  - **Comic:** M. C. Escher, "Ants on a Mobius Strip". Single pane, colored engraving.
  - **Desc:** Nine ants are crawling around 'both' sides of a wire mesh Mobius strip.
  - **Role:** Self-reference leads to global properties (one sided) that violate local properties (two sided).

 ## Chapter 27 - Infinity
  - **Comic:** Frank & Ernest, "Zeno". Single-panel cartoon, black and white.
  - **Desc:** Frank and Eernest as two Greek scholars, "Zeno was late for work yesterday, and wait till you hear his excuse!"

  because you have to go half the remaining distance, an infinite regress.
  Thus motion is impossible (why he couldn't get there, lame excuse), except of course motion is possible - why?
