### World Lines

In relativity, the path of a classical object through spacetime is represented by a line, the *world line*.
This is typically shown in a 2D plane with one dimension of space on the horizontal axis
and time on the vertical axis.
Time is measured in seconds and space is measured in light-seconds.
This places the world lines for photons at 45°.
In the 3D version of this representation, the world lines of photons
create a full cone at 45°, thus the term *light cone*. 

Any two points (events) on such a space-time diagram define a *spacetime interval* 
and will have one of three relationships with each other:
- timelike
- lightlike
- spacelike

In every reference frame, each type of spacetime interval is fixed - timelike in one frame, timelike in all, etc.
There are no reference frames, no super fast movement, that can convert one type of spacetime interval into another.

Normal causality is timelike or lightlike, with the earlier in time event being the cause.
For timelike intervals, the temporal ordering of the two events is an invariant, but the spatial ordering is not.
In different frames, cause and effect can be left-right ordered or right-left ordered.
Timelike causality lies *inside* the light cone.

For lightlike intervals, temporal ordering is invariant, and the spatial separation is constrained to the light cone.
Lightlike causality lies *along* the light cone.

Spacelike causality (if it exists) requires spacelike intervals.
For spacelike intervals, temporal ordering is not invariant, 
while spatial ordering is preserved under boosts along the axis of separation.
In different frames, cause and effect can be either forward in time or backward in time.
Spacelike causality lies *outside* the light cone.

Timelike intervals admit a frame with zero spatial separation, 
while spacelike intervals admit a frame with zero temporal separation.
There is a beautiful symmetry here, which raises the question – "Why no spacelike causes?"

### World Ribbons

The world line is a classical abstraction; it fails to accurately represent the quantum case.
A quantum object does not follow a unique path; there is no fixed trajectory.
The uncertainty principle means that both position and momentum (and thus velocity) are 'fuzzy.'

A better abstraction for quantum systems is the *world ribbon*, basically a fat world line,
but one whose width generally increases over time.
On a spacetime diagram, a world ribbon looks like an inverted triangle.
The edges of the world ribbon are driven by the degree of uncertainty, 
narrow for a tight beam, wider for a less certain object.
The world ribbon evolves unitarily; it is continuous and differentiable.

The problem for the world ribbon concept is what happens when a position measurement is made.
A new triangle starts, but where?
What does the top edge of the world ribbon look like?
Upon measurement, the world ribbon does not evolve unitarily - it collapses, discontinuously.
All we can say is that the top edge of the world ribbon should be spacelike.

Thus, the top edge of a world ribbon becomes a candidate for a spacelike cause.
As a spacetime interval, it is Lorentz covariant; 
it transforms under Poincaré transformations like any other spacetime interval. 
Consistency with special relativity is then merely a constraint on the particular formulation of 'causality.'
