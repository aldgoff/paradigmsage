Let's take a moment to review.

### Consistency

The conventional wisdom is that spacelike causality cannot exist in nature.
The major arguments center on consistency issues generated by self-reference:
- temporal paradoxes (causal contradictions)
- ontological indeterminism (the creation of information out of nothing)

The evidence we just covered suggests that these concerns are overblown
and the arguments are refutable.

### Summary

- Logical paradoxes have been addressed by the addition of imaginary truthvalues.
- Self-reference is a natural way to introduce nonlinearity into quantum systems.
- Closed loops in spacetime consisting of spacelike connections are one way to achieve self-reference.
- Some toy universes have objective measurement mechanisms based on self-reference.
- Modern research programs have developed formal systems of logic that incorporate self-reference.
- Entanglement supports the Ambiguous Collapse Paradigm: counterintuitive, conflicting causal accounts.
- World ribbons illustrate the need for spacelike intervals to make collapse consistent with relativity.
- Symmetric spacetime intervals are one possibility, currently limited to photons.
- Noisy superluminal communication can admit consistent histories that idealized channels exclude.
- Entropy increases with time or with causality; they are degenerate invariants.

### Assessment

The quantum measurement problem has gone unsolved for a hundred years.
Historically, long-unsolved fundamental problems often require a paradigm shift.
Some kind of spacelike causality, sufficient to support self-reference via closed loops in spacetime,
offers a potential paradigm shift.
The paradox-based arguments against spacelike causes do not hold up against an in-depth analysis.
The assumption of noise-free superluminal signaling has been shown to be a critical assumption.
Spacelike causality may be disallowed, but the paradox-based arguments put forward fail to prove this.
General spacelike causality may be too broad, but some restricted variation may be viable, even required.

The QTP hypothesis does not change the idea or the math of unitary evolution.
Instead, it postulates that unitary evolution eventually gives rise to cyclic entanglements,
which impose global constraints that may tease out a locally preferred basis.

It is time to revisit the logical basis for three suppositions: 
- that cyclically entangled systems might self-collapse
- that the details of cyclic entanglement determine the basis of collapse
- that the indeterminism of self-referential forms provides a basis for quantum randomness
