## A Simple Taxonomy of Quantum Systems.
  The behaviour of a quantum system is defined by two processes:
  Penrose calls them
  - U (unitary evolution)
  - R (reduction of the wave function)

### Unitary Evolution (continuous, differentiable, deterministic)
  The formalism of QM and QFT are *linear*.
  The state of a quantum system is specified by a *wave function*.
  The Schrodinger and Dirac equations specify how the wave function evolves in time.
  For a given situation there are multiple solutions, many wave equations that conform to the physics and conditions.
  Any linear combination of theses solutions is also a solution.

### Reduction (discontinuous, not differentiable, stochastic)
  No known mechanism.
  Suspected that something must add a nonlinear element to the quantum formalism.
  An objective measuremement mechanism (OMM) must answer three questions:
  - Q1: When does collapse occur?
  - Q2: In what basis does collapse occur?
  - Q3: How are nonlocal outcomes kept in sync? 

## The QTP Hypothesis
  Self-reference is a powerful, natural way to introduce nonlinearity into a formal system.
  Indeed, it typically cannot be avoided, unexpectedly showing up by surprise, unwanted, and often with catastrophic consequences.

  The QTP hypothesis is that an isolated quantum system will self-collapse when it becomes cyclically entangled with itself (Q1),
  no external environment required (no pesky observer).
  Such cyclic entanglements are causal, being paradoxical in some bases but indeterminate in others, 
  potentially only one - self-selection of a collapse basis (Q2),
  yielding non-deterministic results.
  These cyclic entanglements imply that quantum nonlocality is some variation of a spacelike cause (Q3),
  which keeps distant outcomes self-consistent.

  Justifying this speculation will require deep dives into self-reference, 
  paradox, quantum mechanics, logic, Hilbert spaces, mathematics, paradigms, and philosophy.
