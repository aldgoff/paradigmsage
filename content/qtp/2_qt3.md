### A Toy Universe

*Quantum tic-tac-toe* is a variation on the childhood game of tic-tac-toe
that introduces superposition - every *placement move* consists of two *spooky marks*.

Spooky marks can share squares, and thus moves can become *entangled*.

When an entanglement becomes *cyclic*, a new type of move is required: a *collapse move*.

QT3 is an example of a toy universe with an *objective measurement mechanism* based on *self-reference*.

The self-reference is not causal, but rather relational.

### Limitations

- There is only *one basis* in QT3 (the nine squares, a positional basis)
- there is *no relativity* (the board is a fixed spacetime grid)
- there is *no concept of motion* (the marks are simply placed)
- there is *nothing that oscillates* (no phase)
- the marks are fully *distinguishable*

### Conceptual Gains

Although only one aspect of quantum nature is simulated (*superposition*), 
in this tiny toy universe, the quantum measurement problem has a solution via self-reference; 
an objective measurement mechanism *exists*, and it has a *clear interpretation*.
