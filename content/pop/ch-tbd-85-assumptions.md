**Chapter 85 - Assumptions**

## Portent
  State is an important concept in physcis.
  It is supposed to represent everything that is knowable about a system at some instant in time.
  In a strictly causal system only ignorance of the state makes the future uncertain,
  and the same argument works for the past.
  Given that the laws of physics are time symmetric, in principle, both past and future should be exhaustifly predictable.
  If "instant in time" is a valid assumption, 
  if the system is 'causal',
  if "the laws of physics are indeed time symmetric".
  In such cases one may claim that the past exists, set in the concrete of history.
  
  But what about systems that don't have these properties?
