**Chapter 86 - 3D Chess**

## Portent
  One of the most powerful ways to reveal hidden assumptions is to change the problem.
  Promoting a game by a dimension is an effective way of changing the problem.
  Promoting tic-tac-toe by a dimension is a good example.
  The 3x3 board became a 3x3x3 board, but, oops, now X wins every single game, and it doesn't even matter where his first move is.
  What was the hidden assumption? That a win required a 3-row.
  The board was promoted by a dimension, but the winnning criteria was left unchanged.
  Promote 3-rows to 4-rows, add just one square to the winning condition, and you get Qubic®, a viable 3D tic-tac-toe game.

  It's just a little more challenging to do this for chess.
