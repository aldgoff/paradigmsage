## Portent
  Why do we think that true is different from false?
  Why do we think that horizontal is different than vertical, or diagonal different than slant?
  Why do we think that position is different from momentum, or space different from time?
  Binary bits in digital circuits are assigned different voltages, but the assignment is arbitrary.
  The imaginary number, i, is the square root of minus one, but so is -i.
  We think that +i and -i are different, but in truth we can only tell one from the other by convention.
  Same for the imaginary truthvalues, i & j, convention is all that disambiguates them.
  And if they do indeed form a conjugate basis to the Boolean truthvalues, then we can't tell true from false either.
  If truth and falsehood are perfect forground/background complements of each other then for them too which is which is ambiguous.

  Ambiguity seems rampant at the quantum level.

## Significance
  If a photon can be in eitehr a horizonal or vertical polarization state, 
  or in either a diagonal or a slant polarization state,
  then a digital circuit flip-flop should be able to hold either a true or a false truthvalue, 
  or hold either an imaginary-true or an imaginary-false truthvalue.
